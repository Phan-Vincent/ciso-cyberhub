## Understand the concept

Analyze capability, access, incentives and constraints before assigning an actor label. Financially motivated criminals may buy access; an insider may already have legitimate privileges. Nation-state attribution requires evidence beyond tool names or country-associated infrastructure. Shared tools and compromised servers make simple conclusions unreliable.

## Apply it to a realistic decision

For an unexpected data export, first establish who accessed which resource, when and through which session. Consider both misuse and a legitimate scheduled job. Keep observations separate from hypotheses. Protecting the affected data does not require deciding an attacker nationality.

## Practice and explain

Write two competing hypotheses for a large nighttime export. For each, name a log or owner interview that could strengthen or weaken it.

Write a short answer before checking the flashcard or quiz. Include the evidence you would collect, a likely failure case, and a limitation of your proposed control. Good security work makes assumptions visible and gives another person a way to verify the result.

## Check your understanding

Why separate actor motivation from capability?

**Expected reasoning:** A desired outcome does not prove an actor has the access or resources to achieve it. Shared tooling alone does not establish actor identity or incident scope.
