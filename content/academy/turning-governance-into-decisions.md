## Understand the concept

Governance assigns decision rights, accountability and acceptable risk. Policies establish requirements; standards make them specific; procedures explain repeatable actions; guidelines offer advice. A policy without an owner or exception process often becomes a document rather than an operational control.

## Apply it to a realistic decision

A club may require peer review before publication. The standard defines how many reviewers and which branches are protected. The procedure shows how to open and review a pull request. An emergency exception should name the approver, reason and expiration rather than silently bypass the policy.

## Practice and explain

Draft a one-paragraph publication policy and a three-step procedure. Identify who can approve an exception and how the next cohort will discover it.

Write a short answer before checking the flashcard or quiz. Include the evidence you would collect, a likely failure case, and a limitation of your proposed control. Good security work makes assumptions visible and gives another person a way to verify the result.

## Check your understanding

Why does a policy need an owner?

**Expected reasoning:** Someone must interpret, review, maintain and enforce it. A procedure translates requirements into repeatable operational steps.
