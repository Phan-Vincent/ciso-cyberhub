## Understand the concept

Indicators are clues, not conclusions. Authentication failures, missing logs, unexpected processes and unusual network connections gain meaning through correlation. Password spraying tests a small set of passwords against many accounts; repeated attempts against one account suggest a different pattern. Distinguish an observed event from the interpretation attached to it.

## Apply it to a realistic decision

An impossible-travel alert can arise from stolen sessions or from legitimate VPN exits. Check the timestamps, authentication method, device identity and preceding events. Do not dismiss the alert solely because a VPN is possible, and do not disable an account without considering the response policy and business impact.

## Practice and explain

Use the supplied synthetic event records to identify a failure burst followed by a success. State what can be concluded and what remains unknown.

Write a short answer before checking the flashcard or quiz. Include the evidence you would collect, a likely failure case, and a limitation of your proposed control. Good security work makes assumptions visible and gives another person a way to verify the result.

## Check your understanding

What makes an indicator useful?

**Expected reasoning:** Reliable context, a known collection method, and a testable link to a hypothesis. Spraying spreads attempts across accounts, often to reduce per-account lockouts.
