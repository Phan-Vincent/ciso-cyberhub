## Understand the concept

Classify data according to its sensitivity and required handling, then follow it through creation, use, sharing, retention and disposal. Encryption at rest does not prevent an authorized application from exposing plaintext. Masking changes what is shown; tokenization substitutes a reference whose mapping must be protected.

## Apply it to a realistic decision

A student dataset may be safe for a private class project but unsafe for a public portfolio. Remove real identities and evaluate whether combinations of fields can still identify individuals. Use synthetic examples when the educational objective does not require personal data.

## Practice and explain

Label each copy of a fictional dataset: laptop, backup, log export and shared folder. Explain how deletion and access controls will reach every copy.

Write a short answer before checking the flashcard or quiz. Include the evidence you would collect, a likely failure case, and a limitation of your proposed control. Good security work makes assumptions visible and gives another person a way to verify the result.

## Check your understanding

What risk can remain after obvious identifiers are removed?

**Expected reasoning:** Reidentification through combinations of quasi-identifiers or linked datasets. At-rest encryption does not govern what an application releases through an API.
