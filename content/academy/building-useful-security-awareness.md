## Understand the concept

Awareness programs should help people recognize risky situations and act effectively. Teach a small number of concrete behaviors, provide an easy reporting route and adapt content to different roles and accessibility needs. Measure improvements in reporting and response, not just completion counts.

## Apply it to a realistic decision

A phishing exercise can reveal confusing workflows, not merely inattentive users. Debrief with clear examples and avoid collecting unnecessary personal details. Make legitimate messages and support channels easier to distinguish. A punitive program may discourage the early reports that defenders need.

## Practice and explain

Write a short reporting instruction for a suspicious access request. Test it with a peer who has not seen the process and note any ambiguous step.

Write a short answer before checking the flashcard or quiz. Include the evidence you would collect, a likely failure case, and a limitation of your proposed control. Good security work makes assumptions visible and gives another person a way to verify the result.

## Check your understanding

Why make reporting low-friction?

**Expected reasoning:** Early reports can reduce harm, while confusing or punitive processes discourage them. Reporting behavior and timeliness are closer to the intended security outcome than passive content counts.
