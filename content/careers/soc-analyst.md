## Foundations to build

Develop networking, authentication, endpoint and log-query fundamentals. Practice separating event data from an alert vendor’s interpretation.

Start with the [Security+ foundation lessons](/academy/) and keep a list of concepts you can explain without notes. You do not need to collect certifications before doing small, authorized projects.

## A practical progression

1. Reproduce a small guided task and record the inputs and expected outcome.
2. Change one assumption, predict the result, and test it.
3. Write down a failure you encountered and how you diagnosed it.
4. Ask a peer to repeat your instructions on a clean environment.

Investigate the synthetic logon sequence. Build a small detection test set with positive, benign and missing-telemetry cases. Begin with the [related guided lab](/labs/windows-events/) and explore [sigma](/tools/sigma/) when it supports a specific question.

## Portfolio evidence

Publish a triage report with a timeline, two hypotheses, evidence references and an escalation decision. Include a README, scope, setup, original or properly licensed sample data, verification steps and limitations. Remove credentials, personal data and employer or university information that you are not authorized to share. A smaller project that another person can reproduce is more useful than a long tool list without evidence.

## Certification planning

Research CySA+, SC-200 and Blue Team Level 1 after building a practical logging foundation. Study resources here are free; certification examinations and some external training are not. Treat the [certification planning records](/academy/certifications/) as research prompts, not verified eligibility advice.

## Internships, applications and community

Choose legitimate entry-level roles whose tasks overlap your demonstrated work. Tailor your resume with accurate examples: the problem, your action, the evidence and the result. Do not claim production experience for a synthetic lab. Track applications and prepare a short explanation of one project’s design tradeoff. Club competitions can provide useful team practice when their rules and scope are clear. No path or credential guarantees employment.
