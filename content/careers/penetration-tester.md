## Foundations to build

Learn networking, web requests, operating-system permissions and secure coding before using automation. Practice writing precise findings.

Start with the [Security+ foundation lessons](/academy/) and keep a list of concepts you can explain without notes. You do not need to collect certifications before doing small, authorized projects.

## A practical progression

1. Reproduce a small guided task and record the inputs and expected outcome.
2. Change one assumption, predict the result, and test it.
3. Write down a failure you encountered and how you diagnosed it.
4. Ask a peer to repeat your instructions on a clean environment.

Inspect an owned local web application through ZAP. Document one confirmed issue and its limitations without targeting external systems. Begin with the [related guided lab](/labs/packet-analysis/) and explore [zap](/tools/zap/) when it supports a specific question.

## Portfolio evidence

Publish a lab-only assessment report with scope, evidence, impact, remediation and retest results. Include a README, scope, setup, original or properly licensed sample data, verification steps and limitations. Remove credentials, personal data and employer or university information that you are not authorized to share. A smaller project that another person can reproduce is more useful than a long tool list without evidence.

## Certification planning

Research eJPT, PNPT, PenTest+ and OSCP through official provider pages; exam and training costs are separate from this site. Study resources here are free; certification examinations and some external training are not. Treat the [certification planning records](/academy/certifications/) as research prompts, not verified eligibility advice.

## Internships, applications and community

Choose legitimate entry-level roles whose tasks overlap your demonstrated work. Tailor your resume with accurate examples: the problem, your action, the evidence and the result. Do not claim production experience for a synthetic lab. Track applications and prepare a short explanation of one project’s design tradeoff. Club competitions can provide useful team practice when their rules and scope are clear. No path or credential guarantees employment.
