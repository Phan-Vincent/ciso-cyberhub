## Foundations to build

Develop clear writing, process mapping, asset ownership, risk assessment and evidence evaluation. Learn to distinguish a policy’s existence from its operation.

Start with the [Security+ foundation lessons](/academy/) and keep a list of concepts you can explain without notes. You do not need to collect certifications before doing small, authorized projects.

## A practical progression

1. Reproduce a small guided task and record the inputs and expected outcome.
2. Change one assumption, predict the result, and test it.
3. Write down a failure you encountered and how you diagnosed it.
4. Ask a peer to repeat your instructions on a clean environment.

Build an access-review evidence matrix for a fictional club system. Include control owner, test method and exception expiration. Begin with the [related guided lab](/labs/incident-response/) and explore [openssf-scorecard](/tools/openssf-scorecard/) when it supports a specific question.

## Portfolio evidence

Publish a concise risk register and a control assessment that makes assumptions and scope visible. Include a README, scope, setup, original or properly licensed sample data, verification steps and limitations. Remove credentials, personal data and employer or university information that you are not authorized to share. A smaller project that another person can reproduce is more useful than a long tool list without evidence.

## Certification planning

Research CISA, CRISC and CISM requirements directly; experience requirements and examination eligibility are distinct questions. Study resources here are free; certification examinations and some external training are not. Treat the [certification planning records](/academy/certifications/) as research prompts, not verified eligibility advice.

## Internships, applications and community

Choose legitimate entry-level roles whose tasks overlap your demonstrated work. Tailor your resume with accurate examples: the problem, your action, the evidence and the result. Do not claim production experience for a synthetic lab. Track applications and prepare a short explanation of one project’s design tradeoff. Club competitions can provide useful team practice when their rules and scope are clear. No path or credential guarantees employment.
